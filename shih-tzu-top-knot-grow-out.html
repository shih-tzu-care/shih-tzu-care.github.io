Growing Out a Shih Tzu Top Knot: Week-by-Week Through the Awkward Stage

From clipped forehead to a knot that stays up: the holds, lengths and five-minute routine that get a growing coat through the in-between months.

Home›Blog›Top Knot Grow-Out

That moment when your Shih Tzu's forehead hair is too long to stay out of the eyes but too short to tie up is the real reason most owners give up on a top knot. Growing out a Shih Tzu top knot takes a few months of in-between hair, and what decides whether you get through it is not patience alone but which hold you use at each length. Here is how to get from a short face trim to a tidy knot without poked eyes, broken hair or a dog that rips every band out.

Quick answer: Expect roughly three to four months from a close trim before the coat on top of the head is back, according to a University of Minnesota veterinary dermatology text, and plan on using different holds as it grows: small clips while the hair is short, one soft band once it can be gathered, and two sections once it is thick. Take the knot down and comb it every day, keep a clear line of sight over the eyes the whole time, and trim the eye corners instead of giving up. The planner below tells you which stage you are in today.

Top knot grow-out planner: which stage are you in?

Measure the longest hair on top of the head, from the skin between the eyes and ears to the tip, stretched gently. If you measured two weeks ago too, add that number and the planner estimates your own dog's growth speed instead of guessing.

The planner uses lengths, not dates, on purpose. Hair speed varies from dog to dog and season to season, so a date-only plan tends to be wrong for your dog by the second month. Measure every two weeks with a soft tape and the estimate gets better each time.

How long does it take to grow out a Shih Tzu top knot?

From a close face trim, plan on about three to four months before the hair on top of the head is long enough for a full knot. A practical pet knot can be tied much earlier, once the hair reaches past the eye corner.

The three to four month figure comes from that UMN open dermatology text, which says a dog's coat generally regrows within 3 to 4 months after clipping and that failure to regrow in that time should be considered abnormal. It also notes that in some cases complete regrowth is only seen after 12 to 24 months. That is the clinical side. In everyday terms, it means a closely clipped forehead will look patchy and spiky for weeks before it lies down, and a long show-style knot is a project of many months, not a few weeks.

Puppies are on a different clock

If you are growing out a puppy, the timing is gentler. Shih Tzu breeder Maria Laaksonen writes in a long-coat guide published by The Shih Tzu Club in the UK that at about 4 months of age the forehead hair is long enough to be collected into a top knot. The same guide warns that puppy hair is easy until about 7 to 8 months, that the first tangle phase arrives around 8 to 10 months, and that the difficult phase can last until about two years. In other words, many owners hit the awkward stage of the top knot and the coat change at the same time, which is why so many give up around the first birthday.

When slow regrowth is a vet question

If a shaved patch shows no regrowth at all after four months, mention it at the next vet visit. The UMN text treats missing regrowth past that window as abnormal, and a vet can rule out causes that have nothing to do with grooming.

Weeks 1 to 3: leave it alone, keep the eyes clear

Very short forehead hair is the easy part. It may stick up, but it rarely tangles. Your only job is to keep the area clean and stop single hairs from pointing into the eyes. Our guide on how often to groom a Shih Tzu face covers a daily wipe routine that fits here.

Weeks 4 to 6: the poke-in-the-eye stage

This is the stretch that makes owners reach for the scissors. Hair is long enough to fall forward and touch the eye, but slides out of any band. Small snap clips set a little behind the eyebrow line hold it back for a few hours. Take them out at night so the hair and skin rest.

Weeks 7 to 10: the first real knot

Once you can gather the hair between two fingers above the eyes, try a single soft band. Groomer Kathy Rose, writing in Groomer to Groomer magazine, describes using a child's bungee-type hair band to pull a sparse top knot into one single knot, and calls the single band method the easiest one for owners to do themselves.

Weeks 11 to 16 and beyond: two sections

When one band starts sliding back or the knot flops, the hair is heavy enough for two sections. The same Groomer to Groomer article says that for a full top knot two bands may be required, which is covered step by step further down.

Why does a growing top knot poke the eyes, and does it matter?

Hair at the in-between length is stiff enough to stay straight and short enough to land right on the eye surface. For a breed with large, prominent eyes, that is more than a cosmetic problem.

The Shih Tzu face is meant to have hair going in every direction. The Royal Kennel Club breed standard describes a "chrysanthemum-like" face with hair growing upward on the muzzle, but it also says the hair must not affect the dog's ability to see, and it strongly recommends that the hair on the head is tied up. So a top knot is not just a style; it is the practical answer the breed standard itself points to.

The eye side matters too. The Wikipedia article on the Shih Tzu notes that a common eye problem in the breed is epiphora, excess tearing, which can be caused by fur on the eyelids scratching the conjunctiva and the cornea. If you see squinting, a half-closed eye or a new tear stain during the grow-out, deal with the hair that day and call the vet if the eye stays closed. Trim the corners, keep the top

You do not have to choose between a top knot and clear eyes. Trim the small triangle of hair at the inner eye corner and across the bridge of the nose, and leave the hair on top of the skull to grow. The knot is made from the top of the head, not from the hair right next to the eyes.

Clips, bands or a puppy knot: which hold should you use?

Use clips while the hair is too short to gather, one soft band once it can be gathered, and two bands once the knot gets heavy. Regular office rubber bands are the one option to avoid.

The light yellow bands come from Maria Laaksonen's guide on the UK club site, which says they are advisable for home top knots because they are light and not so tight. Whatever band you use, the rule is the same: you should be able to slide a fingertip under the base of the knot, and the skin around the eyes should not be pulled upward.

Bows and adornments

A bow goes on the band, not instead of it. In Kathy Rose's two-section method, the bow sits on the front band. For show dogs, the Royal Kennel Club standard recommends the head hair is tied up without adornment, so skip the bow in the ring.

Step by step: setting a two-section top knot

This method follows the order described in the Groomer to Groomer drop-coat article and suits hair from roughly the third month onward. Work on a non-slip surface with treats close by.

  1. Comb first. Comb the whole head down to the skin with a metal comb. Never band hair that has tangles in it; the tangle tightens under the band.
  2. Mark the sides. Comb the top knot hair from the outer eye corner back to the ear on each side, so the knot is the width of the eyes, no wider.
  3. Part across the skull. Part across the top of the skull from ear to ear. Everything in front of the part is the top knot.
  4. Split into two sections. Split that hair into a front and a back section across the top of the skull.
  5. Band the front. Secure the front section with a band. It should be fuller than the back section. Check that the dog can blink freely.
  6. Band the back. Gather the back section and secure it to the front section, slightly above it.
  7. Add the bow. If you use one, attach it to the front band.
  8. Check the eyes. Look at the dog face on. No stray hair should touch either eye, and the eyes should look relaxed, not stretched.

Taking it down every evening

The American Kennel Club notes that the hair on top of the head should be combed daily, so it is important to remove and change the top knot each day. Snip latex bands with small round-tip scissors, sliding the blade along the band rather than across the hair, then comb the hair out. Pulling a band out drags hair with it.

The daily routine through the awkward stage

Most of the work is five minutes a day. Once a week, give the whole coat a proper brush-through.

  • Morning (2–3 minutes): comb the top knot hair to the skin, wipe the eye corners, put in the clip or band, check the eyes face on.
  • Midday glance: if the band has slid back or hair is touching an eye, redo it. Dogs that scratch at the band usually do it in the first hour.
  • Evening (2 minutes): cut or unclip the hold, comb out the knot, let the hair and skin rest overnight.
  • Weekly: full brush-through of the coat. The UK club guide says one thorough grooming a week is enough for an older dog, while a young dog going through its coat change needs grooming every two or three days.

If the coat is long, remember that the Shih Tzu's soft double coat, as the Wikipedia entry puts it, tangles and mats easily if not brushed at least every 2 or 3 days, and the base of the top knot is one of the first places to felt. Spread small tangles with your fingers before the brush; the club guide says tangles should never be ripped out with a comb.

When your dog won't keep the band in

Some dogs paw the band out within minutes. Make the first sessions short, put the band in right before a meal or a walk so the dog is busy, and reward the dog for leaving it alone. If the dog mouths your hands during the combing, our grooming biting training plan breaks the handling into small steps. A loose band is better than a tight one the dog fights.

Common mistakes during the grow-out

  • Banding too tight. If the eyes look pulled or the dog keeps scratching, loosen it. Comfort first, style second.
  • Leaving the same band in for days. Hair felts under the band and breaks when you finally take it out. Redo it daily.
  • Making the knot too wide. Hair taken from beyond the outer eye corner pulls the ear hair in and drags on the skin. Stay inside the eye-corner-to-ear line.
  • Cutting the whole forehead "just once". Every full forehead trim restarts the clock. Trim only the eye corners and bridge of the nose.
  • Banding over tangles. A tangle under a band turns into a mat at the skin within days.
  • Giving up at month eight to ten. For a young dog, the coat change makes everything tangle at once. It is a phase, not proof the knot is not working.

When a short practical knot makes more sense

Not every dog needs a long knot. Kathy Rose describes a shorter option in a separate Shih Tzu makeover article for the same magazine: lifting the top knot coat between the fingers and trimming it with blenders to about an inch and a half to two inches, which gives a soft top knot that falls naturally into the ears. Ask your groomer for that if your dog fights bands or you cannot commit to daily take-downs. Grow-out checklist

  • Metal comb that reaches the skin
  • Small round-tip scissors for the eye corners and for cutting out bands
  • A few small snap clips for the poke stage
  • Soft single bands for the first knot, small grooming bands for two sections
  • Soft tape measure and a note on your phone with the length every two weeks
  • Treats within reach for every session
  • A plan for when to stop: squinting, a closed eye or a matted base means a groomer or vet, not another band

FAQ

How long does it take to grow out a Shih Tzu top knot?

From a close trim, plan on about three to four months before the coat on top of the head is fully back; a veterinary dermatology text says clipped coat generally regrows within 3 to 4 months. A small practical knot can be tied earlier, once the hair reaches past the eye corner.

At what age can a Shih Tzu puppy wear a top knot?

A breeder guide on The Shih Tzu Club UK site says the forehead hair is long enough to be collected into a top knot at about 4 months of age.

What should I use to hold back hair that is too short to tie?

Small snap clips placed a little behind the eyebrow line, taken out every night. Trimming the inner eye corners also helps without restarting the knot.

Should I take the top knot out every day?

Yes. The AKC says the hair on top of the head should be combed daily, so the top knot should be removed and changed each day. Cut latex bands out instead of pulling them.

Do rubber bands damage a Shih Tzu's hair?

Thick office rubber bands grip and tear the coat. Use small grooming bands or the light, loose bands a breeder recommends for home knots, and never leave a band in for days.

Why does my Shih Tzu keep pulling the band out?

It is usually too tight or new. Loosen it, put it in before a meal or walk, reward the dog for leaving it alone and keep early sessions short.

Can I keep the top knot short instead?

Yes. A groomer can trim the top knot hair to about an inch and a half to two inches so it falls softly onto the ears, which avoids most of the awkward stage.

The short version

Growing out a Shih Tzu top knot is a length problem, not a willpower problem. Clips while it is short, one soft band once it gathers, two sections once it is heavy, a daily take-down and comb-out, and scissors only at the eye corners. Measure every two weeks, let the planner above tell you which stage you are in, and you will reach the point where the knot simply stays up, usually somewhere around the fourth month.
